Northern Algeria: Meridian enters Africa through Algeria, a country whose music is politically charged. Rai music, born in Oran, was banned actually, under both French colonialism and Algerian conservatism. Khaled’s DiDi reached international levels of recognition in 1992 initially through a Paris based label after Rai music had been suppressed for decades. French colonial administrators found its working class Algerian themes threatening to them, then after independence Algerian authorities found its political side equally as dangerous. An interesting contradiction, freedom music and an authoritarian government. A song from a banned genre became North Africa's most recognizable pop export, is a triumph in its own right. Didi succeeded globally because it passed through Paris, despite suppression. The persuasive architecture of DiDi operates on two levels. For western audiences, it is exoticization. It is fresh, a new sound. The call and response and Khaled’s rasp are foreign enough to be interesting but familiar enough for people to come back and listen again. For Algerian and other audiences, the song's survival is the argument. It is an international pop production that in a way represents resilience, as it managed to gain fame despite suppression.

Song: Didi by Khaled
Track brought Rai to international audiences and is the most recognizable North African song in the world

The absence of film is the point. In October 1863 no one had yet learned to watch the game. The rules were written in a tavern, set in a serif face that would outlive every man who signed them, and handed to a field. The world, for another twenty-one years, had no zero line. That would come later, drawn by the same country, through the same hemisphere, the same month of the year.
02 — Dispatch
From a correspondent in the north · November 1863

The field is not remarkable. It is flat, which is the point. It is marked with lime, which is new. Two sets of men in different colors wait for something to begin. I am told that what is about to begin has rules.

The ball is placed at the center. Everything starts from the middle line that divides the rectangle exactly in half. A man told me before play that this line is called the halfway line. I wrote it in my notebook and could not explain, even then, why it felt important.

They play for an hour in the grey light. Nobody wins by very much. Afterward the men shake hands and go inside for tea. I walk back toward the city. The field is behind me, empty now, and I think: something has been decided here today, though I could not tell you what.

Is It Us?

34 million times each weekday, people board public transportation. 52% of the world’s urban population has convenient access to public transportation, which is approximately 1.9 billion people. Public transportation connects people to their daily lives – jobs, families, and friends – or different worlds – regions, states, and countries. Widening your worldview is made easier by high-speed trains connecting people to each other; you can explore different lifestyles and cultures at the drop of a hat and just go. Lives are made easier and quality of life is higher because of public transportation. Not only is getting around easier, it’s better for the environment. When you take public transportation, that’s one less trip emitting pollutants from gas cars into the atmosphere. It’s something that I wish I had. Coming back to the U.S. was the most disappointing experience of my life. After a month across the world traveling Italy from top to bottom, I had a different expectation of what the world is supposed to be. Ignoring the fact that it’s cleaner, the food is full of less additives, the nature is preserved, and the artifacts are honored, the public transportation is what really got me. In Italy, as is true in most regions in Western Europe and East Asia, you can get virtually anywhere by train or bus. We started in Venice, then to Milan, to Lake Como, Florence, Rome, Naples, Amalfi, and Cinque Terre all by train. At any time of the day, we could access virtually wherever we wanted, conveniently too. It was clean and well run; comprehensive and understandable, despite not knowing the language. We purchased rail passes, and zipped around the country comfortably and easily. I didn’t think much of it in the moment, but looking back I’m so envious of that.
In the U.S., 50% of Americans have no access to transportation. This means that, without a car, half of all Americans are stuck in place or within walking distance, and it’s only half because ~80% of Americans live in urban or metropolitan areas. Cities like NYC and Chicago have subways and trains which allow residents to travel the city, but it basically stops there. I’m from metro-Detroit, and I didn’t ride a public bus in the entire state until I was 18. Now, living in Colorado, I rely on the public transportation bus system to get around. While I’m very grateful for this, I still shake my head because it takes me an hour to get somewhere I could drive to in 15 minutes. I also shake my head because in comparison to Michigan, Colorado public transportation is abundant. I find it absolutely astounding that, for the most part, driving is the only way to access the places you need to go. When I was in Italy, even the most remote parts of the country, such as Cinque Terre, had trains going through them. Here, I’m lucky if there’s a bus stop 30 minutes from me, or at all. The lack and low quality of public transportation in the U.S. creates such a disconnect from county to county and state to state. It’s like we’re completely separate from each other, because I don’t want to drive 10+ hours to get to the next state. It’s not a day trip or a visit to another state or across the country, it’s travel. I could fly to Europe in less time than it takes me to drive back to Michigan. If we can’t be connected as a country, how are we supposed to be “one world?” The difference in public transportation is just one example of the completely different lifestyles that exist across the globe. Internationally, in areas with this level of public transportation, people are more connected to each other and understand more cultures. Someone in Italy can visit France for the day, if they so pleased. They have more access to different cultures, which allows them to understand the world more. That’s something that the American lifestyle cannot accommodate. If you live in the middle of the country, not by Canada or Mexico your chances of crossing a border are very slim, unless you go overseas. Maybe it has something to do with the geography of the planet: we’re sort-of isolated on this western side of the globe. We don’t have many options of countries to visit, even if we did have adequate public transportation. Maybe that’s just not something Americans can experience due to our location on the globe. However, with 50 states, there’s so much to see and learn even within our own borders, but we can barely access them, and definitely not easily. Lifestyles are just so different between other continents and countries and the U.S., to the point where it’s hard to understand each other at times. Transportation in each country is just an aspect of that, because it’s physical proof of the differences in governments, priorities, behaviors, activities, and knowledge of the world around us.
